Glossary & Detailed Explanations

Acronyms, parameters and how each metric is calculated and used in commercial book publishing (Seermai context).

🔤 Key Acronyms

ASP — Average Selling Price: Net revenue ÷ net units. Reflects the real price after discounts.

COGS — Cost of Goods Sold: Direct costs to produce and deliver the book (printing, paper, binding, inbound freight, and often royalties if treated as product cost).

DSO — Days Sales Outstanding: Average number of days to collect payment from retailers/distributors (Accounts Receivable ÷ average daily sales).

EBITDA — Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation & Amortisation: A measure of operating profitability.

HHI — Herfindahl-Hirschman Index (simplified): Sum of squared channel shares; higher values mean greater concentration risk.

KPI — Key Performance Indicator: A measurable value that shows how effectively a publisher is achieving key business objectives.

OPEX — Operating Expenses: Fixed and semi-fixed costs of running the publishing business (salaries, rent, overhead, etc.).

P&L — Profit & Loss statement: The financial projection or actual results for a title or the company.

ROAS — Return on Ad Spend: Revenue generated from advertising ÷ ad spend (e.g. 2.3x means ₹2.30 returned per ₹1 spent).

TACOS — Total Advertising Cost of Sales: Ad spend ÷ total royalties or revenue. Shows advertising burden relative to overall sales.

YoY / QoQ — Year-over-Year / Quarter-over-Quarter: Growth comparison versus the same period in the prior year or previous quarter.

Frontlist — New or recently published titles (typically current season / last 12 months).

Backlist — Older titles still in print and generating sales. Usually higher-margin once advances are earned out.

1 Sales & Revenue Metrics — How they work

Gross Units Shipped — Total copies sent to retailers, distributors or customers before any returns.

Units Returned — Copies sent back by retailers under returnable terms. High returns reduce true sales and inflate apparent demand.

Net Units = Gross Units − Returned Units. This is the real quantity sold to end customers and the base for most royalty calculations.

Gross Billings — Invoice value before discounts, returns and allowances.

Net Revenue (Net Sales Value) = Gross Billings − Discounts & Allowances − Returns Value. The most important top-line figure for publishers.

Average Selling Price (ASP) = Net Revenue ÷ Net Units. Shows the effective price realised after trade discounts.

Return Rate (Units) = Returned Units ÷ Gross Units × 100. Industry caution levels often start above 15–20%; rates above 25–30% are usually problematic.

Format Mix — Split of sales across Hardcover, Paperback, eBook and Audiobook. Helps decide print runs and format strategy.

Channel Revenue — Sales broken down by Online retail (Amazon etc.), Wholesale/Bookstores, Direct-to-Consumer, Libraries, Special sales and Export. Reveals concentration risk.

Frontlist vs Backlist — Share of revenue from new titles versus older ones. A healthy backlist provides stability and better margins.

Sales Velocity = Net Units of a title ÷ Weeks on Sale. Useful for early performance signals and reprint decisions.

2 Profitability Metrics — How they work

Gross Profit = Net Sales − COGS.

Gross Margin % = (Net Sales − COGS) ÷ Net Sales × 100. Many trade publishers aim for 50%+; significantly lower figures often indicate high manufacturing costs, high discounts or high royalty rates.

Contribution Margin = Net Sales − COGS − Direct Costs (marketing, fulfilment, commissions, platform fees). Shows what is left to cover fixed overheads and profit after all variable costs of a title.

Contribution Margin % = Contribution ÷ Net Sales × 100. One of the best single metrics for deciding whether to keep promoting or reprint a title.

Royalty % of Revenue = Author royalties paid ÷ Net Sales × 100. High percentages on underperforming titles destroy overall profitability.

Marketing Spend % of Revenue — Efficiency of promotional investment. Rising spend without matching sales growth is a warning sign.

OPEX % = Total Operating Expenses ÷ Net Sales × 100. Measures overhead efficiency as the business scales.

Days Sales Outstanding (DSO) = Accounts Receivable ÷ Average Daily Sales. Higher DSO means cash is tied up longer with retailers.

3 Returns & Inventory Metrics — How they work

Average Inventory (units) = (Beginning Inventory + Ending Inventory) ÷ 2.

Inventory Turns = Units Sold in period ÷ Average Inventory. Higher turns mean stock is moving faster and less capital is locked in inventory. Typical healthy range for trade books is often 3–5+ turns per year.

Weeks of Supply — Approximate number of weeks the current ending stock would last at the recent sales rate. Helps decide reprints versus remaindering.

Return Rate — As above; also calculated on value. High returns destroy profit and distort demand signals.

Sell-Through % = Units sold to end customers ÷ Units shipped to retail × 100. Low sell-through means stock is sitting in the channel and is likely to return.

4 Costs & Production Metrics — How they work

COGS per Unit = (Printing + Paper/Materials + Inbound Freight) ÷ Print Run + Royalty per unit + Other variable costs per unit. Core unit economic measure.

Fully Loaded Cost per Unit = COGS per unit + (Development / Pre-publication costs ÷ Print Run). Includes editorial, design, typesetting and cover costs allocated across the print run.

Development Cost per Unit — Pre-publication spend spread over the number of copies. Higher on short runs.

Approximate Breakeven Units — Rough number of units needed to recover development costs after covering variable costs (simplified; refine with actual net ASP and contribution).

Schedule Variance % = (Actual production days − Planned days) ÷ Planned days × 100. Positive variance means delay.

Completion Rate % = Titles completed ÷ Titles planned × 100. Measures delivery against the publishing programme.

5 Marketing & Engagement Metrics — How they work

TACOS (Total Ad Cost of Sales) = Ad Spend ÷ Total Royalties or Revenue from the title/period × 100. Lower is better; rising TACOS can indicate advertising is becoming less efficient.

ROAS (Return on Ad Spend) = Revenue attributed to ads ÷ Ad Spend. A ROAS of 3x means ₹3 of revenue for every ₹1 of ad spend.

Marketing ROI = (Attributed Net Sales − Marketing Spend) ÷ Marketing Spend × 100.

Review Velocity = New reviews in period ÷ Number of days. Faster accumulation of reviews improves algorithmic visibility and conversion, especially on Amazon and Goodreads.

Email List Growth % = (Ending list size − Starting list size) ÷ Starting size × 100. One of the highest-ROI assets for launches and backlist promotion.

Series Read-through % = Book 2 buyers (who previously bought Book 1) ÷ Book 1 buyers × 100. Critical for deciding whether paid advertising of a series is profitable.

6 Author, Advances & Rights — How they work

Advance — Up-front payment to the author against future royalties. Unrecovered advances are a pure cost.

Earn-out % = Royalties earned to date ÷ Advance × 100. Shows how much of the advance has been “earned back” through sales.

Unrecovered Advance = Advance − Royalties earned to date (floored at zero). Money that has not yet been (and may never be) recovered.

Expected Recovery % = Expected lifetime royalties ÷ Advance × 100. Forward-looking view of whether the advance is likely to earn out.

Subsidiary Rights Income — Revenue from translation, audio, film/TV, serial and other rights. Usually high-margin and an important part of a title’s total contribution.

Rights % of Book Revenue — Rights income relative to the book’s own net sales; highlights the value of rights exploitation.

7 Channels & Geography — How they work

Largest Channel % — Share of total channel revenue coming from the single biggest channel. High dependence (especially >50–60%) increases commercial risk if that channel’s terms or algorithm change.

Channel Concentration (simple HHI-style) — Sum of the squares of each channel’s share. Higher numbers indicate more concentration.

Domestic vs Export % — Geographic split of revenue. Useful for understanding currency, logistics and rights opportunities.

8 Operational Metrics — How they work

On-time Delivery % = On-time deliveries ÷ Total deliveries × 100. Affects retailer relationships and can trigger chargebacks if missed.

Defect Rate % = Defective or error-related returns ÷ Total units shipped × 100. Quality control indicator; even small percentages matter at volume.

Order Fill Rate % = Fully filled orders ÷ Total orders × 100. Measures ability to supply complete orders; incomplete orders frustrate retailers and readers.

💡 Practical notes for Seermai

All monetary values in this dashboard are expressed in Indian Rupees (₹).

Excel import: Use the Import Excel button in the header with the companion file Seermai_Metrics_Input_Template.xlsx. Yellow input cells from each sheet are loaded into this dashboard and all calculators/charts refresh automatically.

Colour coding on KPI values uses common trade-publishing benchmarks (green = healthy, amber = caution, red = risk). Adjust thresholds to match Seermai’s list size, genre mix (Tamil literature, translations, general fiction, etc.) and distribution model.

Track metrics at both title level and overall list / company level. Title P&Ls that roll up to imprint level are the foundation of good acquisition and reprint decisions.

Leading indicators (review velocity, email growth, early sell-through, ad efficiency) help you act before lagging indicators (final units, profit) are known.

Use the Full Report tab to capture a snapshot after updating inputs; it can be printed or saved as PDF for internal reviews.
